WELCOME TO MAHIYANGANA

Ancient Faith,
Living Heritage

Mahiyangana Raja Maha Viharaya is a historic Buddhist temple located in the Uva Province of Sri Lanka, within the Badulla District. Buddhist tradition identifies this sacred site with the Buddha’s first visit to the island, and it is also counted among the Solosmasthana, the sixteen sacred places of Buddhist pilgrimage in Sri Lanka.

Beyond its religious significance, the temple represents an important part of the island’s archaeological, historical and cultural heritage.

Some accounts of the temple’s early history are preserved in Buddhist chronicles and religious tradition. Traditional accounts should be distinguished from independently documented archaeological evidence.

A JOURNEY THROUGH TIME

The History of Mahiyangana

A religious tradition and cultural heritage preserved across generations.

The Buddha’s First Visit to Sri Lanka

According to Buddhist chronicles, including the Mahavamsa, the Buddha is traditionally believed to have visited Mahiyangana on the Duruthu full moon day, nine months after attaining enlightenment.

Sumana Saman and the Stupa Tradition

Buddhist tradition recounts that the deity Sumana Saman received a sacred hair relic from the Buddha and enshrined it in an early stupa at this site.

Royal Patronage

Chronicles and historical accounts describe periods of restoration and development of the stupa and temple under the patronage of successive rulers.

Preserving the Heritage

Today, religious observances and pilgrimage continue while the temple’s historic, archaeological and cultural heritage remains an important part of Sri Lanka’s Buddhist tradition.

CULTURE • DEVOTION • TRADITION

The Mahiyangana Esala Maha Perahera

The annual Perahera associated with Mahiyangana Raja Maha Viharaya and the Mahiyangana Maha Saman Devalaya is a distinctive cultural and religious celebration, bringing together devotion, traditional Kandyan dance, drumming and Sri Lanka’s living cultural heritage.
